Case study

Stock Move Explainer

Watches a stock watchlist daily, and when a price drops past a threshold, explains why using only the company’s own SEC filings: grounded, cited, and honest when nothing fits. Architected, built, tested, and shipped end-to-end.

View source

The problem

AI stock explainers usually just make it up

Ask most AI tools why a stock moved and you get a fluent, confident paragraph with no way to check it against anything real. This project takes the opposite approach: it never lets an LLM invent a cause. It retrieves the company’s own risk disclosures, hands the model only that retrieved text plus the price move, and requires it to say so honestly when nothing in the filings plausibly explains what happened, rather than force a connection that isn’t really there.

The build

Retrieval-grounded explanation, not a guess

Two daily jobs. Ingestion checks SEC EDGAR for new 10-K/10-Q filings, extracts each one’s Risk Factors section, chunks and embeds it, and stores it in pgvector. The trigger job pulls daily closes from Alpha Vantage, and on a qualifying drop, runs a similarity search scoped to that company, sends the top matches plus the price move to an LLM, and emails the result. The email includes every retrieved source passage, not just what the model chose to cite, so the explanation is checkable by the reader, not just a claim.

Price drop→ pgvector retrieval→ Grounded LLM→ Cited email

My role

Directed and built end-to-end

  • Directed the full architecture (a layered pipeline: adapters, domain logic, data access, orchestration) and every design decision, including stack choices.
  • Made the call to restrict the trigger to price drops only, since Risk Factors text can’t plausibly explain an upward move, and required the LLM to say “no clear connection found” rather than force one.
  • Caught and fixed real bugs surfaced only by testing against live systems, not just reviewing code: a broken package install that silently worked locally but would have failed under cron, two distinct Risk Factors extraction failures found on a real Microsoft 10-K, a pgvector type mismatch blocking retrieval entirely, and a wrong CI action version.
  • Built and reviewed the grounding eval suite against real LLM output, checking not just whether the model reached the right verdict but that every quoted citation was an exact, verbatim match to the source text.
  • Worked in an AI-assisted workflow (Claude Code). The architecture, the design decisions, and the review of every change were mine.
